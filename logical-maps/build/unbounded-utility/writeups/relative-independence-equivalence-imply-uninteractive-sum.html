Relative Expectation, Mixture Independence and Stochastic Equivalence imply Uninteractive Sum Invariance

Proved implication: Rich Outcomes + Stochastic Equivalence + Mixture Independence + Relative Expectation imply Uninteractive Sum Invariance. Stochastic Dominance, Totality, Shift Invariance and every continuity principle are left out. Together with the converse uninteractive-sum-equivalence-imply-relative, Uninteractive Sum Invariance is equivalent to Relative Expectation under DU.

Proof: Claude (Fable 5.1), 10–11 October 2026, in response to Zachary Goodsell’s request for a minimal set of principles entailing the principle he proposed on 6 October 2026. No independent check or Lean verification is claimed.

Setting

Gambles are real-valued random variables on the standing atomless space, with identity utility; all joint laws are available. Write M(X,W) for the fair mixture M_{1/2}(X,W), realized as X on \{B=0\} and W on \{B=1\} for a fair coin B independent of the gambles mixed; the two mixtures compared in one application of Mixture Independence use the same coin. This is how the randomized-selection construction is used throughout the map.

Two variables X,Z are uninteractive when E\min(|X|,|Z|)<\infty. The principle to prove: if (X,Z) and (Y,Z) are uninteractive then X\succeq Y\iff X+Z\succeq Y+Z.

Step 1: integrable common summands cancel

Lemma 1 (Relative Expectation + Mixture Independence). If E|Z|<\infty then, for every coupling of Z with (X,Y), X\succeq Y\iff X+Z\succeq Y+Z.

Proof. By Mixture Independence with the common component Y+Z,

X\succeq Y\iff M(X,\,Y+Z)\succeq M(Y,\,Y+Z).

On the same coin,

M(X,Y+Z)-M(X+Z,Y)=(1-B)(X-X-Z)+B(Y+Z-Y)=Z(2B-1),

which is integrable with E[Z(2B-1)]=E[Z]\,E[2B-1]=0 because B is independent of Z. Relative Expectation therefore gives M(X,Y+Z)\sim M(X+Z,Y). The same computation gives M(Y,Y+Z)\sim M(Y+Z,Y). Finally, Mixture Independence with the common component Y gives M(X+Z,Y)\succeq M(Y+Z,Y)\iff X+Z\succeq Y+Z. Chain the four statements by transitivity. \square

With Z constant this is Shift Invariance. Nothing about laws was used.

Step 2: separability over an event

Lemma 2 (Mixture Independence + Stochastic Equivalence). Let A be an event with 0<P(A)<1. If X^\circ,Y^\circ vanish on A and Z vanishes off A, then X^\circ\succeq Y^\circ\iff X^\circ+Z\succeq Y^\circ+Z.

Proof. The recorded result independence-to-sure-thing gives Sure-Thing. The gambles X^\circ+Z and Y^\circ+Z both equal Z on A, so their restrictions to A are identical, hence indifferent. Sure-Thing with the event A says X^\circ+Z\succeq Y^\circ+Z iff the restrictions to A^c compare the same way, and those restrictions are X^\circ and Y^\circ. \square

This is the only place where Stochastic Equivalence is used.

Step 3: the reduction

Let (X,Z) and (Y,Z) be uninteractive, so m_X=\min(|X|,|Z|) and m_Y=\min(|Y|,|Z|) are integrable. Put

A=\{|Z|>|X|\}\cap\{|Z|>|Y|\}.

On A, |X|=m_X and |Y|=m_Y, so X1_A and Y1_A are integrable. On A^c, |Z|\le|X| or |Z|\le|Y|, so |Z|1_{A^c}\le m_X+m_Y and Z_s=Z1_{A^c} is integrable. Write Z_b=Z1_A.

If P(A)=1, X and Y are integrable, so X-Y and (X+Z)-(Y+Z)=X-Y are integrable and Relative Expectation decides both comparisons by the sign of E[X-Y], with indifference at zero. If P(A)=0, Z=Z_s is integrable and Lemma 1 finishes. Otherwise let p=P(A)\in(0,1) and

a=E[X1_A],\qquad b=E[Y1_A],\qquad \gamma=\frac{b-a}{1-p},\qquad X^\circ=X1_{A^c},\qquad Y^\circ=(Y+\gamma)1_{A^c}.

All these gambles exist in the real chart by Rich Outcomes.

Left side. X-(X^\circ+a)=X1_A-a is integrable with mean zero, so X\sim X^\circ+a by Relative Expectation. Likewise Y-(Y^\circ+a)=Y1_A-\gamma1_{A^c}-a has mean b-\gamma(1-p)-a=0, so Y\sim Y^\circ+a. Hence X\succeq Y\iff X^\circ+a\succeq Y^\circ+a\iff X^\circ\succeq Y^\circ, the last step by Lemma 1 with the constant -a.

Right side. Lemma 1 removes the integrable Z_s: X+Z\succeq Y+Z\iff X+Z_b\succeq Y+Z_b. The same two replacements apply, since (X+Z_b)-(X^\circ+Z_b+a)=X1_A-a and (Y+Z_b)-(Y^\circ+Z_b+a)=Y1_A-\gamma1_{A^c}-a, so X+Z\succeq Y+Z\iff X^\circ+Z_b\succeq Y^\circ+Z_b.

Closing. X^\circ,Y^\circ vanish on A and Z_b vanishes off A, so Lemma 2 gives X^\circ\succeq Y^\circ\iff X^\circ+Z_b\succeq Y^\circ+Z_b. Combining the three displays, X\succeq Y\iff X+Z\succeq Y+Z. \square

Which premises are needed

  • Mixture Independence. Extend the base area preorder cdf-area-preorder by declaring W\succeq V whenever W\mathrel{R}C and 0\mathrel{R}V for a standard Cauchy C, where R is the area comparison. This is a preorder satisfying CDF-Area Extension (every new pair has infinite positive area, so no finite-area comparison is added), hence Relative Expectation and Stochastic Equivalence, but C\succeq 0 while C+1\not\succeq 1, so Shift Invariance fails and so does the conclusion. Mixture Independence fails there.
  • Some continuity. total-exact-ultrafilter satisfies DTU and fails Shift Invariance, so Mixture Independence and Stochastic Equivalence alone do not suffice.
  • Shift Invariance with Sure-Thing is not enough. finite-shift-total-extension satisfies DTU and Shift Invariance but fails Shift Transfer. Uninteractive Sum Invariance implies Relative Expectation (the converse result), which gives Shift Transfer at once: M_p(X+b/p,Y) and M_p(X,Y+b/(1-p)) differ by a bounded mean-zero variable. So that model fails the conclusion.
  • L¹ Continuity is not needed. lexicographic-folded-extension satisfies DTU and Relative Expectation but fails L¹ Continuity; the conclusion holds there by this result.

Consequence for fixed copulas

Call a copula corner-avoiding when it gives zero mass to ([0,\delta)\cup(1-\delta,1])^2 for some \delta>0. If a pair (X,Z) admits such a copula, it has the joint law of (Q_X(U),Q_Z(W)) with (U,W) distributed as the copula, and on each of the two regions one quantile is evaluated inside [\delta,1-\delta], where it is bounded. So the pair is uninteractive, and Uninteractive Sum Invariance gives \operatorname{SC}(C) for every corner-avoiding C. The adjacent-quarter shuffle copula used in the clipped-expectation write-ups is corner-avoiding with \delta=1/4; its direct verification there is now a special case of this result together with those models’ Relative Expectation.
