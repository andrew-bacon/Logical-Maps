Full action models: all transformations with their natural action

Source construction: Andrew Bacon, Substitution Structures (2019), §4 and §5.3, especially the D^D example on p. 46 of the author-hosted draft of 10 January 2019. Translation to relational action-model intensions and additional verdict arguments by OpenAI Codex, prompted by Andrew Bacon, 28 October 2026. General full-model arguments cite Classicism §3.5 and Appendix D separately. No Lean verification is claimed for these entries.

Construction

Let X have at least two elements. Set M = X^X with ordinary function composition, D_e = X, and f acting on an individual x by f(x). Propositions are P(M), with

f\cdot p=\{g:g\circ f\in p\}.

Higher types are full M-set function spaces, restricted to the map’s relational types. Equivalently a relation is an arbitrary family of extensions indexed by transformations; transport shifts that family by composition and transports its arguments. Truth is evaluated at the identity transformation. All relational constants of Σ denote top.

There is no finite-support restriction and no symmetry restriction. In particular the natural action on X is not replaced by a trivial action on fixed individuals. The two records take X = {0,1} and X = N. The following basic arguments work for any X with at least two elements, in ZFC.

Barcan fails at both e and t

For either type σ, write f_σ for its transport map. Fullness supplies a property H_σ with extension im(f_σ) at transformation f. Every root entity x necessarily satisfies H_σ, because f_σ(x) lies in im(f_σ).

Choose a constant transformation k with value a. At type e its image is {a}, so an individual b distinct from a at the target does not satisfy H_e. At type t, the identity and k are distinct, but

1\circ k=k\circ k=k.

Every proposition k·p therefore contains either both 1 and k or neither. The proposition {1} at the target is outside the image of k_t. Thus in both types the premise of BF is true at the identity and its conclusion is false. At each input type the same H_σ and the constant-top property also witness failure of Functionality; p = top witnesses failure of Tractarianism. BF-at-e has no separate map node, so its witness is retained in the argument for failure of the all-type schema; BF-at-t is a separate verdict.

B and other failures

Take p = {1}. It is true at the identity. After k it is impossible, because no h has h composed with k equal to 1. Thus B fails. The same observation shows that k transports both p and bottom to bottom, refuting necessary distinctness at t; at e, k identifies distinct individuals. The repository’s unretracted-arrow argument records the t failure and derives failure of B.

The constant map is not epic (1 and k agree after k), so the existing nonepic-strong-leibniz argument refutes the Strong Leibniz Biconditionals at t. A transposition is a nonidentity returning arrow, so the existing argument refutes Distinctness-Preserving Collapse. Consequences of these failures are computed by the map.

Positive verdicts and Atomicity

Fullness gives boxed Rigid Comprehension and boxed Atomicity at every relational type. In particular D_t = P(M) has singleton atoms. These models are not counterexamples to Atomicity. Every relational entailment algebra is likewise a full powerset of arrow/argument tuples, so arbitrary intersections give Boolean Completeness, at every object. The singleton {1} is the strongest truth at the sole object; hence Actuality holds there and at the target of every arrow, giving boxed Actuality.

The existing full-model arguments give Relational Choice (using choice in the metatheory), and extensional fullness gives Transversal Choice. The one-object argument gives No Pure Contingency; this also supplies boxed forms via the map’s recorded implications where applicable. The entries are in ZFC: no choiceless variation is asserted.

Why two instances

For X = {0,1}, there are four transformations and sixteen propositions. The finite-arrows argument gives boxed Intensional Choice; the finite propositional-domain arguments refute the infinity principles at t. A map with a left inverse is invertible on this finite carrier.

For X = N, the individual and propositional domains are infinite. The existing extensional-fullness arguments give the two Axioms of Infinity and the corresponding infinity schemata. Successor has a left inverse but is not onto. It has distinct left inverses (choose different values at 0), separated by full propositions, so the existing retraction arguments settle further principles, including failure of Strong Actuality. These facts must not be transferred to the finite instance.

Both instances also satisfy boxed Intensional Choice: the general intensional-choice-well-ordering argument applies because full domains contain all codomain-dependent intensions (using ZFC). Thus this verdict for the infinite instance does not rely on the finite-arrows argument. The current map closure settles all 139 recorded principles for each of these two instances (78 true and 61 false for the two-element carrier; 76 true and 63 false for the natural-number carrier). These are counts for the records of 28 October 2026, not claims about every expressible higher-order principle. The executable check checks/bacon_model_witnesses.py exhausts the two-element monoid’s low-type witnesses; it is not a formalization of the full type hierarchy.
