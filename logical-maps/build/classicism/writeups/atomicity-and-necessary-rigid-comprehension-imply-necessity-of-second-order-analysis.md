# Atomicity ∧ □Rigid Comprehension ⇒ Necessity of Second-Order Analysis

<p class='cert'>Result — Source: Could the truths of mathematics have been different? (draft); produced by Andrew Bacon (Theorem 5.2, proved as Theorem C.2 in Appendix C.2); read into the map's formulation by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), prompted by Zachary Goodsell, 23 October 2026; recorded by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), prompted by Zachary Goodsell, 23 October 2026.</p>

## Premises

- **Atomicity.** Every non-bottom entity of each relational type has an atom below it.
- **□Rigid Comprehension.** Necessarily, every relation, including a proposition, is coextensive with a rigid one.

## Conclusion

- **Necessity of Second-Order Analysis.** Every second-order analytical sentence is either true in every real number structure in every possible world or false in every real number structure in every possible world. One sentence for each type $\sigma$ and each second-order analytical sentence.

## Proof

Bacon's Theorem C.2. Its argument: the canonical real number structure, built from the finite numerical quantifiers (Theorem A.2), rigidified by Rigid Comprehension, is an inflexible real number structure, its elements being necessarily distinct relative to the necessity $\Box_N$ that assumes a possible natural number structure (Lemma C.4); with the Leibniz Biconditionals and Rigid Comprehension it is necessarily complete, hence necessarily a real number structure, and its rigid subproperties form a rigid property, by the pullback of possible rigid subproperties through world properties that the map records as atomicity-and-rigid-comprehension-imply-rigid-power (Lemmas C.5–C.7); the induction of Lemma C.1 extended to second-order sentences then makes every second-order analytical truth of it necessary; and any real number structure, at any type, agrees with it on second-order analytical sentences (Lemma C.3). Hence, for each such sentence $A$, $\Box_N\forall\mathfrak R(\operatorname{Real} \mathfrak R\to A(\mathfrak R))$ or the same with $\neg A$, and $\Box_N$ may be replaced by $\Box$ because a real number structure contains a natural number structure, so the matrix is vacuous where none is possible. The map's second-order quantifiers range over all relations where Bacon's range over rigid properties; under □Rigid Comprehension every relation agrees on the structure's elements with a rigid one at every world, and analytical formulas do not distinguish them (Proposition C.3), so the two readings of each instance are necessarily equivalent.

## Notes

Bacon's Corollary 5.1, the same conclusion from C5 and □Rigid Comprehension, is derivable on the map: □Rigid Comprehension gives □Actuality, which with C5 gives Atomicity. The argument proves more than the sentence schema, namely Second-Order Analytic Necessitism at the type of the canonical reals; the schema is what it yields at every type, through Lemma C.3.
