# Necessity of Second-Order Analysis ⇒ Non-contingency of the Continuum Hypothesis

<p class='cert'>Result — Source: Misc.; produced by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), prompted by Zachary Goodsell, 23 October 2026; recorded by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), prompted by Zachary Goodsell, 23 October 2026.</p>

## Premises

- **Necessity of Second-Order Analysis.** Every second-order analytical sentence is either true in every real number structure in every possible world or false in every real number structure in every possible world. One sentence for each type $\sigma$ and each second-order analytical sentence.

## Conclusion

- **Non-contingency of the Continuum Hypothesis.** Either necessarily every real number structure satisfies the continuum hypothesis, or necessarily every real number structure falsifies it: that every subproperty of the continuum either injects into the natural numbers or is in bijection with the continuum. One sentence for each type $\sigma$.

## Proof

$\operatorname{CH}(\mathfrak R)$, that every subproperty of $X$ injects into $N$ or is in bijection with $X$, is a second-order analytical sentence (Background, Analysis): its quantifiers range over elements of $X$, over subproperties of $X$ and over the relations witnessing injections and bijections. The Non-contingency of the Continuum Hypothesis at $\sigma$ is the instance of the Necessity of Second-Order Analysis at $\sigma$ and that sentence.
